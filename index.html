Elitsa Yotkova

I study why AI judges get it wrong — and try to build ones you can trust.

MSc student in Information Retrieval & Knowledge Discovery, Sofia University  ·  based in Sofia, Bulgaria

Email CV Google Scholar GitHub LinkedIn

I work on the reliability of LLM-as-a-judge systems — how to evaluate open-ended model outputs correctly, why judges disagree with humans (and with each other), and how to design benchmarks that actually expose those failures instead of papering over them. Alongside my MSc, I'm a research assistant on the SUMMIT project at Sofia University, and I've recently interned on Google Search's AI Mode team in Zurich and built observability infrastructure at Uber in Amsterdam. I also teach — I've led six courses to 775+ students since 2022.

Take part in SemEval‑2027

SemEval was where my own research on LLM behavior started — our stylometric code-detection system competed in SemEval 2026 Task 13. The 21st edition, SemEval‑2027, just opened its 11 shared tasks, spanning reasoning & inference, lexical semantics, cross-lingual understanding, framing & stereotype detection, and — especially relevant to what I work on — agent risk evaluation and multi-hop tool-calling. If you're looking for a shared task to cut your teeth on or to test a new idea against a real leaderboard, I'd genuinely encourage you to look through the SemEval‑2027 task list and sign up. And if you end up tackling one of the LLM-evaluation or agent-related tasks, I'd love to hear about it — reach out.
